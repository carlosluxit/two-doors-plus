Hurricane Impact Windows vs. Hurricane Shutters: The Honest Comparison

For most South Florida homeowners, impact windows are the better long-term investment. Shutters are cheaper upfront but require manual deployment before every storm and add no value between storms. Impact windows are 2–4× the upfront cost of accordion shutters but deliver UV protection, noise reduction, security, energy savings, and 65–85% resale value recovery — with zero storm prep.

TL;DR

Impact windows win on convenience, daily benefits, security, noise, energy, resale, and stress. Shutters win only on upfront cost. Both qualify for the same insurance discount if NOA-approved and properly installed. If you can afford it, choose impact windows.

What hurricane shutters do well

Lower upfront cost

This is the headline reason most homeowners choose shutters. A typical South Florida home can be fully protected with accordion shutters for $7,000–$15,000 — roughly half to a third of an impact window install. For homeowners on a tight budget who need code compliance immediately (e.g., to satisfy an insurance non-renewal or new build code), shutters can be the right answer.

Faster installation

Most shutter installs take 2–4 days. Impact window replacement takes 3–6 days for a typical home, plus permit time. If a storm is coming next week and you need protection now, shutters are the only practical option.

Easier retrofit on irregular openings

Shutters (especially storm panels) can be added to almost any existing opening with no structural modification. Some old or unusually-shaped openings would need re-framing for impact window installation, adding cost.

What impact windows do well

Always protected — no storm prep

This is the single biggest practical difference. With shutters, you have to physically deploy them before every storm warning — pulling panels out of the garage, climbing ladders, closing accordion shutters, lowering roll-downs. With impact windows, you do nothing. You can be out of town when a storm hits. You can be physically unable to deploy shutters and still be protected.

Continuous daily benefits

Between storms (i.e., 99% of the year), shutters provide zero value. Impact windows actively work every day:

  • UV protection: The PVB interlayer in laminated impact glass blocks 99%+ of UV, dramatically reducing fading of furniture, floors, and artwork
  • Sound reduction: Typical 25–50% reduction in outside noise — meaningful on busy streets, near airports, or on the water
  • Energy efficiency: Insulated double-laminated construction reduces cooling energy use 20–35% versus single-pane windows
  • Security: Laminated glass does not shatter into shards — it holds together when struck, making forced break-in significantly harder than with shutters retracted

Resale value

South Florida MLS listings consistently highlight "full impact windows" as a positive feature. Florida appraisal data and Realtor surveys put resale value recovery at 65–85% of project cost. Shutters typically recover 15–35% because buyers view them as a one-purpose, somewhat dated solution.

Aesthetics

Impact windows look like normal windows. Accordion shutters add visible vertical tracks on either side of every opening. Roll-down shutters add a horizontal housing above each opening. Many HOAs and historic districts (e.g., Coral Gables) restrict or prohibit visible shutters.

Insurance and code peace-of-mind

The insurance discount is technically equivalent if shutters are NOA-approved and cover every opening, but in practice we frequently see shutter installs that miss small openings (kitchen vents, second-story windows, French doors with side lights) which voids the discount. Impact windows are a single uniform solution.

When shutters still make sense

There are scenarios where shutters remain the right choice:

  • You're on a hard budget and need code compliance now
  • You own a rental property where resale-value uplift goes to the owner, not the tenant
  • The home has unusually-shaped openings (large arches, stained-glass features) where impact window cost would be prohibitive
  • You're in a non-HVHZ county where partial protection plans (front-of-house only) are accepted by code
  • The home is on the market and you need a minimum-cost code-compliance solution before sale

Combination approach: Some Miami Beach and Fort Lauderdale waterfront homes use impact windows on all openings plus roll-down shutters on the largest sliding doors as a redundant Category 5 layer. Total cost is roughly $30k–$60k more than impact alone, but the resale-value uplift in luxury waterfront markets often recovers it.

Frequently asked questions

Are impact windows better than hurricane shutters?
For most South Florida homeowners, yes — same insurance discount, no storm prep, daily UV/noise/energy benefits, and significantly higher resale value. Shutters win only on upfront cost.
Do shutters give the same insurance discount as impact windows?
For Miami-Dade NOA approved shutters covering every opening, yes — both qualify as "opening protection" under the OIR-B1-1802 form. Partial-coverage shutters do not qualify.
Can I keep my old shutters after installing impact windows?
Yes. Many homeowners leave roll-down or accordion shutters in place as a redundant second layer. They don't increase the insurance discount (you already get the maximum credit from impact windows), but they're optional belt-and-suspenders protection.
Which shutter type is cheapest?
Aluminum storm panels at $7–$15 per square foot installed, but they require manual deployment and offsite storage between storms.
Which shutter type is most convenient?
Motorized roll-down shutters — closed with a button press, no ladders, no panel handling. Cost roughly half of impact windows ($45–$80/sq ft).
Do impact windows break in Category 5 hurricanes?
NOA approved impact windows are tested to resist large missile impact at design wind speeds equivalent to a Category 5 hurricane (180+ mph) plus an additional 50% safety factor. Documented post-storm performance after Andrew, Wilma, Irma, and Ian has been excellent.
